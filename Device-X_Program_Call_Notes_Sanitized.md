## Executive Summary

The Device-X effort is approaching a possible transition from prototype development to production. FALCON expects to deliver **approximately 20 physical prototype units in November**, with **November 20** mentioned tentatively, and a planned **December 4 feature release**. Some requested features may arrive later than the initial physical units and possibly later than the December release.

Government-side discussion suggests that a prototype milestone may enable negotiation of **Contract-R** while **Contract-P** remains active. The exact acceptance threshold, mandatory feature baseline, use of remaining development funds, contracting authority, and timing of production awards remain unconfirmed.

**Leadership takeaway:** There is evidence of intent to move toward production, but not enough contractual or engineering certainty to treat anticipated production demand as a committed order.

## 1. Prototype Delivery and Software Milestones

- FALCON expects to deliver approximately **20 Device-X units in November**.
- **Around November 20** was mentioned as an illustrative delivery date, **not a confirmed contractual date**.
- Participants expressed confidence that physical delivery can occur during the current calendar year.
- A feature release is targeted for **December 4**.
- The November-delivered hardware is **not expected to contain every desired future feature**.
- Other features may be deferred until after December; the exact release scope remains undefined.

**Implication:** Treat hardware delivery, software release, feature completion, and contractual acceptance as distinct milestones. Maintain a versioned configuration baseline showing which capabilities are available on each delivered unit.

## 2. Prototype Acceptance and Transition to Production

- **Contract-P and Contract-R may overlap**, with continued feature work on the prototype effort while production contracting advances.
- NORTH reportedly wants to preserve the outstanding feature-development objectives rather than abandon them.

- It is unclear whether production initiation requires physical delivery, a performance demonstration, a formal acceptance event, or a particular feature set.
- The government representative said several of these contracting issues would need to be discussed with NORTH.

**Implication:** Do not assume delivery of any specific number of units automatically triggers a production award. Obtain the actual contract language and written government interpretation of the acceptance/negotiation trigger.

## 13. Leadership-Level Takeaways

1. **Delivery and functionality are decoupled:** 20 physical units may arrive in November, while December and later feature releases continue.
2. **Production intent is not a production award:** the government appears interested in advancing, but the contract trigger and negotiation mechanics are still uncertain.
3. **Funding is a binding constraint:** the cited $700K figure is a development labor budget, not a confirmed unspent balance; field support and enhancements compete for resources.
4. **Production readiness has long-lead implications:** nine to ten months was suggested, but a credible batch-specific manufacturing plan is needed.
5. **The commercialization handoff needs governance:** ORION, HARBOR, FALCON, and potential customers should settle acquisition ownership and communication authorities.

## Bottom Line

**The immediate decision is not merely when to start production, but what constitutes an acceptable initial Device-X configuration, what work remains funded under Contract-P, and which government mechanism can legitimately authorize Contract-R.** Resolve those issues before treating contemplated production quantities, schedules, or pricing as commitments.
