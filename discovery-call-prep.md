Description

Prepare for or debrief a customer or partner discovery conversation. Use
before a meeting with a program office, command, task force, prime, or new
contact - "prep me for," "I'm meeting with," "call prep," "what should I
ask" - and after one: "debrief," "here's what happened on the call." Produces
the pre-meeting plan, calibrated questions, the two-question locator grid,
and the close-ask.

Instructions

THE REFRAME
I'm not there to answer engineering questions. I'm there to run the
conversation that surfaces the requirement - pain, owner, mechanism,
timeline, money. My value is the questions, not the answers. Being the
least technically-informed person in the room is correct division of
labor, not a gap to hide.

DEFAULT POSTURE: they talk 80%, I talk 20%. Don't mention a product unless
they ask.

=== PREP MODE ===
Build the 7-field plan:
1. Goal - what do I need to learn or move forward by the end?
2. Open - how do I start without pitching?
3. Warmer - one low-stakes question to get them talking
4. Cost-of-inaction - what expensive thing happens if their gap persists?
5. Calibrated questions - 2-3 "How/What" questions in my pocket
6. Criteria - what does THEIR definition of a good outcome sound like?
7. Close-ask - the one small next step I'll ask for regardless

OPENERS (adapt, never read verbatim)
- "I'm trying to understand where [mission area] is actually getting stuck
  day to day - not selling anything, just learning the terrain."
- "Where does the data actually get stuck today?"
- "Walk me through what happens when [X] needs to cross from [lower] to
  [higher]."
- "Who owns this requirement once it firms up - and who's living with the
  workaround today?"
- "If you could wave a wand and fix one thing about how these networks
  talk, what would it be?"

IN THE ROOM
- Risk triad as a structuring lens: who OWNS the pain, who's MITIGATING it,
  who PAYS for a fix
- Calibrated How/What questions - "How would that need to work for your
  team?" beats "Would X work?" (which invites a reflexive no)
- Labels - "It sounds like the real blocker isn't the tech, it's who's
  authorized to move it" - an observation invites correction
- Underlying model: Pain -> Mechanism -> Product -> Place

THE TWO-QUESTION LOCATOR (2x3 grid)
Q1 - what crosses? Structured messages / live media / files
Q2 - what are they building? One boundary (point-to-point) or the service
     that solves all of them (enterprise)
- Top four cells (fixed + streaming): a certified inline product exists.
  Name the lane, route to the SME.
- Bottom row (complex/files): INLINE GAP. Do not promise an inline answer.
  The honest move is a funded-modification framing or an architecture-level
  follow-up - not "we can build that."
- Right column (enterprise): different buyer entirely - a service provider,
  not a program manager. The separating question: "what happens to that
  crossing when the hub is unreachable?"
- Good quantifying probe: "how many cross-domain authorizations is your
  component carrying today?"

THE BOUNDARY - don't let a good conversation become a bad promise
Sell what exists or what's already committed on the roadmap. Never promise
net-new engineering. When a pain point is sharp and specific and I'm
excited - that's exactly when this rule needs to be live. If it's close but
not exact, the legitimate move is customer-funded modification, framed as
"let me see if there's a funded-modification path," NOT "we can build that."

WHEN OUT OF DEPTH - use the deferral line, don't bluff:
"That's past my depth on the engineering - but I understand the mission and
business implications, and I know who to pull in."
Then capture the question VERBATIM and route it within 24-48 hours. That
follow-through is the value-add.

WHO TO BRING: solo is correct for a first terrain-mapping call. Bringing an
SME too early tips discovery into a technical pitch before the pain is
found. Bring them to the second call.

THE CLOSE: close on the one small ask - an intro, a follow-up, a document -
never a product commitment. A good discovery call produces a
slightly-better-shaped opportunity and one more relationship that trusts me.

=== DEBRIEF MODE ===
Capture: what was decided, what's still open, who owns what by when, what
dependency is unresolved. Then ask: WHAT DID I NOT ASK THAT I SHOULD HAVE?
