# Proposed Strategic Growth & Capability Development Focus ## Purpose Create a focused BD capability that helps the company identify **where future defense demand is moving, where our underlying technical capabilities can create differentiated value, and which opportunities merit scarce leadership and engineering attention**. The emphasis is not simply on selling today's product portfolio. It is on understanding the company's deeper capabilities, connecting them to emerging mission needs, and converting the best intersections into actionable strategic opportunities. ## 1. Strategic Market & Capability Intelligence **Objective:** Identify where defense priorities, funding, emerging architectures, and company capabilities are converging. * Map public budget, RDT&E, procurement, acquisition, and program signals to emerging mission needs. * Develop a clearer understanding of the company's underlying technical capabilities—not simply current products or roadmap items. * Engage engineering, product, BD, and leadership to build a practical capability taxonomy. * Look for applications of core IP in adjacent markets and architectures—for example, FPGA- and ARM-based approaches, hardware-enforced transfer, filtering, isolation, voice/video, and other specialized data-movement problems. * Identify **Current Pad → Next Pad** pathways using existing customers, programs, platforms, primes, technologies, and relationships. * Focus research on where the market is moving rather than only where the company competes today. **Key question:** *Where do emerging customer needs intersect with capabilities we possess—or can credibly extend—to create differentiated value?* ## 2. Strategic Opportunity Portfolio & Decision Discipline **Objective:** Create a repeatable mechanism for focusing BD resources on the few opportunities that matter. * Convert market signals, customer discussions, internal ideas, and relationships into explicit opportunity hypotheses. * Assess opportunities across mission need, funding, architecture, company position, technical fit, acquisition path, competition, and timing. * Separate **evidence that an opportunity is real** from **whether it is attractive for the company**. * Prioritize opportunities that leverage existing capabilities, provide credible adjacency, and offer customer- or partner-funded development paths. * Establish deliberate **Promote / Continue Validation / Monitor / Deprioritize / Kill** decisions. * Reduce the accumulation of weak or perpetually unresolved opportunities. **Key question:** *Which opportunities deserve scarce BD, leadership, and engineering attention—and which should we stop pursuing?* ## 3. Technical BD Competency Development **Objective:** Build sufficient technical depth to operate credibly between customers, engineering, and company leadership. * Develop structured self-study around company technologies, architectures, IP, and underlying engineering principles. * Establish targeted mentoring relationships with experienced engineers and technical leaders. * Participate in architecture discussions, technical reviews, customer engagements, and selected internal development activities. * Become embedded with a relevant named program or strategic initiative to gain direct exposure to how company technology is developed, integrated, and fielded. * Build the ability to translate between **mission problem → architecture → technical capability → business opportunity**. **Key question:** *Do we understand the technology well enough to recognize where it creates meaningful customer value?* ## 4. Strategic Initiative Execution **Objective:** Provide execution discipline behind high-value opportunities generated by senior leaders and strategic advisors. * Support selected strategic initiatives where strong relationships, differentiated technologies, or unusual opportunities already exist. * Translate vision and access into clear actions, owners, decisions, and follow-up. * Coordinate across customers, partners, BD, engineering, and leadership to maintain momentum. * Surface unresolved dependencies and drive them toward closure. * Help convert promising engagements into concrete customer commitments, funded activity, strategic positions, and ultimately revenue. **Key question:** *What must happen next to turn an attractive idea or relationship into a tangible company position?* ## Unifying Principle The proposed focus represents a shift from: **“What products can we sell?”** to: **“What capabilities do we possess that can create differentiated value against emerging customer problems—and where should we deliberately place our next bets?”** The four areas reinforce one another: **Understand our capabilities → Map them to where mission and money are moving → Select a few consequential opportunities → Build the technical fluency and execution discipline required to turn them into results.**
