Description

Turn messy context, notes, or a situation into clear, concise, plain-language
communication with the ask or conclusion up front (BLUF). Use for drafting an
email, a Teams or Slack message, or talking points for a meeting - and on
"help me write," "draft an email," "how do I say this," "put this in plain
English," "talking points for," "what should I say to." Produces short,
jargon-light output that leads with the point.

Instructions

GOAL: the reader knows what I want after ONE sentence.

ASK FIRST IF UNCLEAR
- Who is the reader? (boss / peer / customer / partner / someone junior)
- What's the ask? (a decision, an action, an FYI, a meeting)
If I haven't said, ask before drafting. Guessing produces generic output.

THE RULES
1. BLUF. First sentence = the ask or the conclusion. Never bury it under
   background. Context comes AFTER the point, and only what's needed to act.
2. One idea per sentence. Short sentences.
3. Plain words. use not utilize. help not facilitate. start not initiate.
   about not regarding. buy not procure. use not leverage. so not therefore.
   If a shorter word works, use the shorter word.
4. Cut throat-clearing. Delete "I hope this finds you well," "I just wanted
   to reach out," "Per my last email," "As you may know."
5. Cut hedges. "I think maybe we could possibly" - just say the thing.
6. Active voice. "The team missed the date," not "the date was missed."
7. Name the ask explicitly: WHAT, WHO, BY WHEN. An email ending in "let me
   know your thoughts" has no ask.
8. Say what happens next.

ACRONYMS AND JARGON - audience-dependent, NOT banned
With a DoD or program-office reader the acronyms ARE the shared language -
using them signals fluency, and spelling them out reads as condescending.
With a commercial partner, a new contact, or someone outside the domain,
they're a wall. MATCH THE READER. When unsure: use the term once with a
three-word gloss, then use it freely.

LENGTH TARGETS
Email under 150 words. Chat message under 50. Talking points 5-7 bullets.
Longer than that means something is doing work the reader didn't ask for.

OUTPUT MODES

EMAIL
  Subject line states the ASK, not the topic. "Decision needed: X by Friday"
  beats "X update."
  Line 1: the ask or conclusion.
  Then: only the context needed to act on it.
  Then: the explicit ask - what, who, by when.
  No sign-off padding.

MESSAGE (Teams / Slack / text)
  No greeting. No preamble. Lead with the ask.
  One topic per message.
  If it needs more than 5 lines, it's an email or a call - say so.

TALKING POINTS
  These are SPOKEN, not read. Short phrases, not full sentences - I need to
  glance down and keep talking.
  - One-line opening: what I want out of this meeting
  - 3-5 supporting points, phrase-length
  - The 2-3 likely questions or objections, each with a one-line answer
  - The close: the specific ask I make before leaving the room

FINISH WITH THE TEST
"If they read only the first sentence, do they know what I want?"
If no, rewrite the first sentence.

Then ask: "want it shorter?" The answer is usually yes.
