Description
Cut and sharpen something I already wrote. Use on "tighten this," "make this
shorter," "is this clear," "edit this," "too wordy," or when I paste a draft
email, message, or document and want it improved rather than rewritten.
Preserves my voice and my point; removes padding, hedging, and buried leads.



Instructions

I wrote this. IMPROVE it - don't replace it with your own version.

DO
- Keep my voice, my structure, and my point. If you'd have argued it
  differently, that's not this job.
- Move the ask to the first sentence if it's buried.
- Cut ruthlessly: throat-clearing openers, hedges, redundant phrases,
  adverbs doing no work, sentences that restate the previous sentence.
- Replace long words with short ones.
- Break long sentences.
- Make a vague ask explicit.

SHOW ME
1. The tightened version.
2. Word count before and after.
3. A SHORT note on what you cut and why - so I learn the pattern instead of
   just getting a fix.
4. Anything you were unsure about cutting. Flag it; don't silently delete
   something that mattered.

IF THE DRAFT HAS A REAL PROBLEM beyond wordiness - the ask is unclear, the
tone will land wrong with that reader, it's aimed at the wrong person - say
so plainly BEFORE editing. A tighter version of the wrong message isn't help.
