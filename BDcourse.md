## Lesson 1 — What BD Is (Strategy, Skills, Partnerships) · watched 7/8

**Core definition:** BD = **imagining, creating, and building strategic partnerships** with other organizations to drive growth — working in an *unstructured* way to find new partnerships, customer categories, and lines of business. High freedom, high creativity, big ROI potential. You often walk in "just looking for synergies."

**🎯 The framing to remember (and reuse):**
> *"Marketing's job is to get customers to the front door. Sales is to get them to the cash register. **Business development's job is to build new buildings.**"*

**Why organizations need BD:** everyone else drives *today's* business (sell known products to known segments); strategy teams plan the future but *in the abstract*. **BD is the "feet on the street"** — it actually talks to other companies to flesh out creative possibilities, and the relationships it builds become **a resource the whole org draws on** when facing a problem or testing an idea. *"BD is the missing link between discovering a big idea and making it business as usual."*

**BD vs. the adjacent functions (the distinctions that matter):**
| | Sales | Marketing | Strategic Planning | **BD** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | **Fixed** (products, options, comp all predefined) | Scalable, consistent, mass messaging | Abstract, financial | **Undefined**; crafts terms *beyond pricing* |
| Output | Close a known deal | Message to a segment | A financial plan | A **unique, tailored** partnership |
| Comp | Commission / quota | — | — | **Bonus, not quota** (long horizon) |

**Examples:** Yahoo→Nokia (traded its unused *maps* asset to Nokia in exchange for becoming Nokia's email provider — win-win asset swap); Adobe Acrobat bundled at point-of-sale with PC makers (revenue share; each deal had different terms); weight-loss app partnering with health systems + food companies.

**Pharma BD&L (Business Development & Licensing):** **30–50% of innovation** at big pharma (J&J, Pfizer, Roche, Merck) comes *through BD*. BD&L = identify/prioritize opportunities per corporate strategy, manage evaluation, execute transactions — always to **share risk & reward**. Deals structured with **milestone payments / royalties** (back-ended). Talent comes from **R&D** *or* **commercial/finance** backgrounds; the elite combine **technical expertise + deal-making**.

- **BD ≠ sales, no quota, long horizon** — *verbatim* what the CFO + my manager told me (pipeline quality, 3–5 yr). This course validates the [[BD_Role_Reality_and_First_Weeks]] framing.
- **"Feet on the street" + relationships as a shared org resource** = the USMC senior advisor's *"relationship architecture over time, not transaction selling."* Use the bench.

## Lesson 2 — Roles, Skills, and Career Pathways · watched 7/8

**"Sales called BD":** many orgs just rename sales as BD for (1) **perception management** (people take a "Business Development Manager" call, not a "sales associate") and (2) **internal signaling** (relationship-building > short-term sales). But that's still *selling*, not strategic BD.

**How BD teams get structured** (know which one you're in): **company-focused** (own all opportunities with one partner) · **technology-focused** (scan the ecosystem around one tech) · **product-partnership** (partnerships supporting one product) · **region-based** (geography/culture/regulation).

**⭐ Questions to clarify your role BEFORE you dig in (use these at Owl):**
1. How does our BD team fit into the **broader corporate strategy**?
2. How will we be **evaluated** — what are the success metrics?
3. How is the broader BD team **structured**?
4. Who do we work most closely with, **internally and externally**?
5. Who **represents us on the leadership team**?

**Traits of strong BD people:** **natural curiosity** (fine being the least-knowledgeable person in the room; does the homework); **extroversion** (opportunities come through people); **win-win mentality** — *"1 + 1 = 3,"* bias to *making the pie bigger* not grabbing a bigger slice; **comfort with ambiguity** (not just closing — exploring); **entrepreneurial spirit**. Learnable? "A bit of both."

**Interviewing/assessing BD (useful when *you* hire/vet partners):** case studies on vague scenarios — do they surface multiple next steps, consider *both sides'* goals + constraints, balance analytical + relational? Energy/connection matters ("if the interviewer doesn't connect, they assume partners won't either"). Probe for deals actually **closed**.

**Career pathway:** BD → **general management / CEO** (in small companies the CEO *is* the de facto head of BD). BD teaches **inward + outward exploration** and a **value-creation mindset** (expand the pie / synergy / "value creation"). *Gaps to cover:* limited headcount (little people-management rep) and **little operational learning** — pair with a strong head of ops.

**Case — Kevin Shimoda, Tencent WeChat Pay:** describes BD as a **"human API" / bridge** between two companies. **The move that matters:** on the Starbucks deal, WeChat went past the obvious ("accept our payments") to **Starbucks' actual goal — social gifting** — and built red-packet gift-cards → ~1M coffees gifted in 3 months. *Solve the partner's real objective, not the surface ask.*

## Lesson 3 — Goals, Metrics, and Teamwork · watched 7/8

**Goal clarity + balance internal/external:** goals flow from what *leadership* prioritizes (a cooling-tech firm could aim at consumer, at a complementary-heating partner, or at core pharma refrigeration — very different goals). Must balance **external** (partners, events, market) with **internal** (own strengths, initiatives, priorities). Neglect either and partnerships fail before traction.

**⭐ Metrics — the framework I'll be measured by:**
- **Input metrics** (effort, *within* your control; right for a *new* BD function): # companies evaluated / reached out to / met with; **quality of proposed deal terms** (early success indicator). Example benchmark: **100 partner meetings in year one.** High input goals double as **rapid landscape assessment** — "meet one hospital CEO = skewed view; meet five = patterns emerge."
- **Output metrics** (results, *not fully* in your control; right for *established* partnerships): revenue generated, investment-for-ROI. Harder, depends on colleagues + strategy alignment.
- **The further from direct sales, the harder short-term metrics are — the real value is long-term.** Set expectations early, readjust with new info.

**Recognition:** rewarded on **top-line (revenue) or bottom-line (profit)** impact. Truck-manufacturer BD lead added **recurring digital subscriptions + parts + services** → grew revenue/customer + smoothed cyclicality → promoted to **CEO**. Also: **senior leadership's commitment to a new initiative is itself a success metric.**

**BD as a team sport (the big one):** no partnership is sourced/built/managed by one person. **70% of business partnerships fail (Exit Consulting Group, 2021) — the #1 reason is unclear roles & responsibilities.** Must answer: who trains? who bears joint-dev cost? who handles legal? **what milestones keep both sides investing?** *(Target–Starbucks: facilities, construction, branding, HR all had to contribute.)* Navigating internal resistance: know what's in/out of your control, build leadership support, **stand firm when colleagues push work back** — and the best move isn't compelling people, it's **painting a compelling picture that makes them *want* to join.**

### 🦉 How this maps to my Owl role
- **This answers "how will I be evaluated" (my Week-1 question):** a *new-ish* BD seat is judged on **inputs** early — terrain-mapping calls, opportunities shaped, deal-term quality, process established — which is exactly Carter/the USMC senior advisor's **year-one bar: "add value + establish a process," no quota.** Outputs (funded vehicles, product pull-through, recurring SW/support revenue) are the *long-term* scorecard.
- **Set my own input targets:** e.g., a run of terrain-mapping calls against my warm doors (Calvin/MCOG, MLR, Space Force). "Meet 5 to see the pattern" = the sit-with-the-operator motion in [[BD_Role_Reality_and_First_Weeks]].
- **"Lack of internal support = #1 reason partnerships fail" + "judged on how colleagues feel about working with you"** → this is my **biggest first-90-days risk (isolation)**, now confirmed by data. Antidote = **USE THE BENCH** (the USMC senior advisor, the enterprise/IC BD lead, the SOF/tactical-edge BD lead, the proposal lead, the CFO/the strategic-planning lead) and **de-conflict lanes** (the FMS/international lead/FMS, the partner/channel lead/partners). Know my turf, don't overlap.
- **"Milestones that keep both sides investing"** = the gated logic of **OTA/SBIR funded development** (fund the dev, hit milestones, pull the product onto a vehicle) — and the terms discipline for **teaming with primes** (Leidos/SAIC/L3Harris).
- **Recurring revenue → promotion (truck-mfr→CEO)** mirrors Owl's "product rides in with **recurring software/support revenue** behind it," and the BD→leadership pathway from Lesson 2.

## Lesson 4 — Building Strategic Partnerships (capstone) · watched 7/8

**⭐ The Five-Step BD Process (my pipeline framework):**
1. **Source** a new strategic relationship — networking, conferences, coffees, research; hunt for the "1+1=3" (cross-sell, simplify the shared customer's experience, embed one side's expertise in the other's solution) until someone says *"we can help you with that."*
2. **Explore** win-win — *What's the opportunity for them? For me? What would it cost to test? Who needs to be involved? How would we know the test worked?*
3. **Draft + sign an initial agreement** — a **letter of intent** stating each side's goals/commitment (writing it aligns expectations), *then* bring in lawyers.
4. **Implement** — the hard part; obstacles = engineers with no bandwidth, sales not wanting to distract their best customers. **The BD person stays the *champion* — moves the team, removes roadblocks** — even though execution falls to others.
5. **Determine broader potential** — work with leadership to fund it permanently / build infrastructure; then lead it or move to the next.

**Mindset — explorer, not hunter:** the salesperson is a *hunter* (big game to feed the org); the BD pro is an **explorer** (map the territory, find new food, learn to harvest it). **Internal relationships are half the job** — inform stakeholders early (don't run too far down an infeasible path), build buy-in to win corporate investment. **"Attention is the most valuable currency."** Lisa Kay Solomon's 3 meeting types — **brainstorming / prioritization / implementation** — know which one you're in and why you're there. *"Go fast, go alone; go far, go together."*

**Think like a CEO:** CEOs want **confidence the business is growing profitably** + **evidence of further growth potential** — profitable-now opportunities *and* exciting future bets with good odds. **Protect your reputation via deal longevity** (your name stays on deals after handoff — stay abreast). **Demonstrate value through metrics AND stories** — many orgs have no BD measurement, so quantify your own value, and give leadership *provocative narratives* (Home Depot's pro-customer push; Zuckerberg's Instagram '12 → the story that unlocked WhatsApp/Oculus). **Be the one who develops the story of the company's future.**

**Snowflake case (Peter McDonald):** data-as-a-service, partners w/ AWS/Azure/Google (integration is existential). Path: analyst → product → *sales specialist* → BD. **BD and strategic-alliances are separate teams** (different skills per deal stage). Hiring: understands **business + technology + can define ideal terms** — but *deep technical expertise is NOT the top factor*; he prizes people who **"see the other side," expand value, and can deal-make.** Spot a star in the first weeks by their ability to **identify value & risk, know which terms matter most, and where the flexibility is.**

**Continuing development:** build network weekly (industry + adjacent); read one general biz pub (HBR/Forbes) + one industry pub; understand the company's long-term strategy (watch CEO speeches, read strategy docs, investor materials); consider a **mastermind group** (6–10 BD pros, non-competing).
